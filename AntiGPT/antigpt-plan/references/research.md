# Research Control

Research exists to improve a decision, a model of the system, or an experiment.

## Before expanding a research branch

Name the consequence of the answer:

- Which decision can change?
- Which working model can be falsified or refined?
- Which experiment can become discriminating?

If the answer changes none of these, the branch has low information value.

## Working models

Use explicit, falsifiable simplifications when they help reason about the system. Omitted effects are acceptable when they are unlikely to change the conclusion at the required analysis level.

Record the assumption that matters and the observation that would overturn it. Avoid cataloguing every conceivable limitation.

## Experimental work

Prefer experiments that distinguish competing explanations or choices. An experiment that only produces more observations without changing a decision path is weak.

## Stop condition

Stop gathering information when:

- the current decision is stable across plausible remaining uncertainty;
- the next step is cheap to revise;
- another experiment will not discriminate between live alternatives; or
- the requested research result has already reached a useful synthesis.

Return to the original question immediately after a substantial research branch. State what changed in the decision or model.
